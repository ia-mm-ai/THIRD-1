# CONTINUITY

> **Continuity relation invariant**
>
> Every claimed continuation must identify the exact earlier and later positions, the bearer or bearers occupying them where established, the Continuity Criterion and version, the selected respect, the actual dynamic bridge or declared observational gap, the required preserved distinctions, admitted transformations and losses, the Domain, ordered Interval, Resolution, evidence coverage, and the exact established, negative, or unresolved result. A name, identifier, State, hash, copy, record chain, Profile, source claim, or successor declaration never creates the continuity it represents.

> **Identity firewall**
>
> Strict numerical identity remains Ontology's `SAME`. It is not criterion-relative and is never redefined by this layer. `CONTINUES_AS` is criterion-bound and does not entail `SAME(x, y)`. `SAME`, `DIFFERENT`, persistence, continuation, equivalence, copying, replacement, succession, derivation, lineage, provenance, uninterrupted activity, and recorded history remain distinct.

> **Open-continuity invariant**
>
> Not every way an Existent persists, ceases, divides, combines, sleeps, repairs, regenerates, migrates, or returns is already understood or nameable. Every encountered continuity question may remain located without being denied, fictionalized, forced into numerical identity, or assigned to the nearest familiar criterion merely because the present grammar cannot resolve it.

> **Historical no-erasure invariant**
>
> Continuity preserves relation across change; it does not rewrite the change. Restoration of an earlier Configuration, reconciliation of branches, replacement of a carrier, constitutional succession, correction of a Record, or later discontinuity cannot make an actual intervening Occurrence never have happened. Ending preserves the actuality and attributable history of what preceded it.

## CT0 — Continuity Position

### CT0.1 Continuity Function

Ontology states what Identity, History, Copy, Provenance, Lineage, Beginning, Ending, Relation, Bearer, State, and Process mean. Structure states how endpoint bearers, Systems, relations, Configurations, positions, parts, Boundaries, and realization bases are arranged. Dynamics states what actual changes, copy crossings, forks, reconciliations, suspensions, resumptions, recoveries, and endings occur. Continuity states **what remains related across those actual changes, under which exact criterion, with which preserved distinctions, transformations, losses, gaps, and uncertainty**.

Continuity owns **criterion-bound persistence and continuation across change**.

It does not:

- redefine strict numerical identity or make `SAME` criterion-relative;
- infer `SAME` from continuation, similarity, equivalence, copied State, a shared source, or one identifier;
- infer continuation from the same name, address, account, key, hash, filename, version label, Profile, role, or status;
- make a Continuity Criterion, Profile, Description, Record, graph, pointer, receipt, or emitted turn into the relation it represents;
- turn an actual dynamic transition into continuity merely because it completed or succeeded;
- restrict continuity to successful, authorized, final, accepted, or value-producing change;
- erase failed, refused, partial, invalid, nonconforming, rolled-back, orphaned, or superseded Occurrences from History;
- infer discontinuity from an observation gap, missing Record, inactivity, unavailability, inaccessibility, suspension, or uncertainty alone;
- infer persistence from uninterrupted activity, a heartbeat, repeated execution, availability, consensus, finality, or record append alone;
- collapse bearer continuity, Process continuity, relation continuity, role continuity, State continuity, formal continuity, institutional continuity, Agent continuity, or Subject continuity into one question;
- make a Copy its source, turn source deletion into identity transfer, or make multiple branches one numerically identical bearer;
- make a merge result numerically identical to multiple mutually distinct sources;
- make replacement, repair, restoration, reconstruction, migration, replay, or re-execution preserve or destroy continuity without an applicable Criterion;
- create constitutional Adoption, Currentness, Succession, Carry, Authority, Consent, Right, Obligation, Ownership, Responsibility, jurisdiction, or lawful Effect;
- make physical, computational, or descriptive continuation validate a constitutional Act;
- convert origin, authorship, source position, predecessor position, contribution, control, sponsorship, or lineage into privilege, entitlement, ownership, or value;
- establish life, cognition, Self, Subject status, Agency, Experience, consciousness, intelligence, meaning, adaptation, resilience, coherence, collective capacity, or value;
- treat a dense lineage, many blocks, many turns, many descendants, or long duration as emergent significance by counting alone.

Continuity may remain unresolved even where every relevant dynamic Occurrence is known. An actual continuation may obtain without a complete Description or surviving Record. A perfectly formed continuity model may refer to nothing actual.

### CT0.3 Layer Boundary

The Reality Model remains:

    SUBSTRATE -> ONTOLOGY -> CONSTITUTION -> STRUCTURE
                -> DYNAMICS -> CONTINUITY -> EMERGENCE

The linear notation states semantic dependency, not universal chronology. A continuity relation may concern a past interval, a present interval, or a projected hypothetical case. It still depends semantically on typed endpoints and change, not on when the assessment is written.

- **Substrate** constrains every physically realized bearer and bridge.
- **Ontology** owns the root Kinds, strict identity, History, Copy, Provenance, Lineage, Beginning, and Ending.
- **Constitution** owns normative validity, Currentness, institutional succession, constitutional Carry, binding, and legal or protocol authority where applicable.
- **Structure** owns the arrangement and realization of endpoints, Systems, parts, relations, positions, and mappings.
- **Dynamics** owns actual change, maintenance, transition, copying Occurrences, branching Occurrences, reconciliation, suspension, resumption, recovery, and ending Occurrences.
- **Continuity** owns criterion-relative persistence and continuation assessments, exact preservation and loss across bridge histories, descriptive replacement, copying, branching, merging, succession, dormancy, recovery, dissolution, ending, and lineage relations.
- **Emergence** owns whether continuity relations and histories realize memory, life, Self, cognition, Agency, Experience, intelligence, meaning, adaptation, resilience, coherence, collective capacity, contribution, or value.

Continuity can tell Emergence what remained, changed, divided, combined, slept, returned, or ended. It cannot decide what that history means or is worth.

`WELL_FORMED` requires:

1. the inherited Dynamic Scope is well formed;
2. every endpoint reference is internally typed and projects the inherited Scope coherently;
3. the extent's source, receiving, intermediate, and unresolved-role sets form
   an exact typed partition of the declared endpoint domain, except an
   explicitly combined source-and-receiver endpoint may occupy both of those
   resolved sets;
4. ordering and interval segmentation are sufficient for the asserted Resolution;
5. selected respects are explicit rather than hidden inside a result;
6. the bridge basis distinguishes actual history, observational gap, negative bridge Claim, and unresolved bridge basis;
7. every alleged discontinuity is separately typed and placed;
8. evidence coverage is explicit and does not exceed its source coverage;
9. repeated World, Domain, Interval, or Resolution fields are equal projections or declared sub-scopes;
10. no field presupposes the continuity result being assessed.

The extent is exact only where endpoint IDs and edge IDs are unique; the domain
of `endpoint_reference_ids` equals exactly the IDs in `endpoint_references`;
each source, receiving, resolved intermediate, and unresolved-role ID belongs
to that domain and agrees with the endpoint's declared role; and every known
edge key equals its `edge_id`. Every known edge source belongs to the union of
the declared source and intermediate sets; every known edge receiver belongs
to the union of the declared receiving and intermediate sets. For a resolved
topology, every declared external source is the source of at least one edge,
every declared external receiver is the receiver of at least one edge, and
every intermediate is incident to at least one incoming and one outgoing edge.
The source and receiver sets may intersect only through an endpoint explicitly
typed `SOURCE_AND_RECEIVING_ENDPOINT`. No endpoint may remain outside the four
sets, and no endpoint may occupy a set incompatible with its declared role.

Every topology other than `TOPOLOGY_UNRESOLVED` requires nonempty source and
receiver sets, a nonempty edge inventory, no unresolved-role endpoint, and no
unresolved topology position.

Topology cardinalities are closed as follows:

- `ONE_TO_ONE`: exactly one source, one receiver, and one directed edge;
- `ONE_TO_MANY`: exactly one source, at least two receivers, and exactly one
  edge from that source to each receiver, with no other edge;
- `MANY_TO_ONE`: at least two sources, exactly one receiver, and exactly one
  edge from each source to that receiver, with no other edge;
- `MANY_TO_MANY`: at least two sources and two receivers, with every source and
  receiver incident to at least one edge; a Cartesian product is not required;
- `ORDERED_CHAIN`: the first ordered endpoint is the sole external source, the
  last is the sole external receiver, every interior endpoint is intermediate,
  and the edge inventory contains exactly one directed edge for each adjacent
  ordered pair and no other edge;
- `BOUNDED_GRAPH`: the endpoint and edge domains are finite, every edge is
  internal to the endpoint domain, and every endpoint is incident to an edge;
- `TOPOLOGY_UNRESOLVED`: known endpoint and edge domains remain exact;
  unresolved endpoint roles occur exactly in `unresolved_role_endpoint_ids`,
  and unresolved cardinality, omitted-as-unknown endpoint, or edge positions
  occur exactly in `unresolved_topology_positions`. Either set may be empty
  only if the other is nonempty. A known edge may still be recorded, but no
  resolved cardinality or role conclusion follows from this topology token.

Every topology aggregate supplies exactly one Candidate reference and exactly
one result reference per edge. A not-evaluated or unresolved reference remains
explicit. A duplicate, omitted, extra, or foreign endpoint or edge key is
`ILL_TYPED`.

For each edge, the Candidate and result references carry the same source ID,
receiver ID, bridge reference, and Continuity Scope as the edge. The result
reference names `CONTINUITY_ASSESSMENT` and `ContinuationResult` for that exact
Candidate. A mismatched, cross-scoped, or unbound reference cannot satisfy the
edge and is `ILL_TYPED`.

A well-formed Continuity Scope establishes no endpoint actuality, identity, continuation, lineage, or conformance by itself.

### CT0.5 Canonical Continuity Judgments

The central relation assessment is:

    Gamma |-CT CONTINUITY_ASSESSMENT(candidate_k, profile_p, CS) => e

where:

- `Gamma` is the attributable declarations, Evidence, methods, observations, assumptions, and upstream results;
- `candidate_k` is one `ContinuityCandidateReference` whose referent is typed by CT1 without answering the question;
- `profile_p` is one exact `ContinuityProfileReference` whose referent and version are typed by CT1;
- `CS` is one well-formed Continuity Scope;
- `e` is either an external `ILL_TYPED` result produced before substantive evaluation or one CT2-owned `ContinuationEvaluation` envelope;
- the CT2-owned evaluated envelope contains the dimensional result and exactly one criterion-bound continuation posture.

This subsection registers the judgment shape only. `ContinuationEvaluation` is
a non-semantic forward result label here; CT1 owns Candidate and Profile
referents, and CT2 owns substantive evaluation and the complete result family.
No CT0 inference depends on those later definitions. A
`ContinuityAssessmentReference` refers to an evaluation elsewhere; it is never
the operator's direct return type.

The central assessment is edge-atomic: one invocation evaluates exactly one
ordered source endpoint to one receiving endpoint. Branch, merge, chain, and
graph operators invoke it once for every declared directed edge and only then
evaluate the topology. No single identity, continuation, or temporal posture may
stand for several heterogeneous edges.

The general conformance judgment is separate:

Every substantive schema named `...Evaluation`, other than the nonrecursive `ContinuityApplicabilityEvaluation`, specializes this envelope. A displayed specialized schema may show only its typed payload; the common fields remain mandatory. `NOT_EVALUATED` is a procedural posture, not a substantive result. Each `T` owns its unresolved member. `ILL_TYPED` is returned before an envelope is constructed and is never a member of `T`.

Every repeated payload projection is constrained by the envelope. A same-target
actuality, realization, bridge-history, or coverage field must equal the envelope
projection. A subordinate dimension must name its narrower target and mapping;
its coverage may not exceed envelope coverage. Endpoint maps are total over
exactly the endpoint set relevant to the invocation: an omitted key, extra key,
contradictory repeat, or untyped target projection is `ILL_TYPED`.

Every endpoint-actuality key equals the embedded endpoint ID and bearer term in
the corresponding endpoint reference. `ACTUALITY_ESTABLISHED` requires an
established `EXISTS` judgment for that exact bearer, Domain, and Interval;
Claims and Records are supporting evidence, not the judgment itself.

`RELATION_ACTUALITY_ESTABLISHED` requires an actual `RelationInstance` and an
exact established `EXISTS + RELATES` judgment for the displayed relation kind,
ordered relata, Domain, and Interval. A Claim, Record, Description, graph edge,
or represented posture cannot directly occupy the judgment reference.

A substantive single-Criterion evaluation uses
`SINGLE_GOVERNING_CRITERION`. Composition or expressly multi-Criterion
conformance uses a closed mapped set. Every Criterion use equals the envelope
Profile, Scope, selected respects, and application result. A list of possibly
unrelated Criteria is not a governing binding. A positive result asserting an
obtaining Continuity Relation requires `RELATION_ACTUALITY_ESTABLISHED`.
`RELATION_ACTUALITY_BINDING_NOT_APPLICABLE` is legal only where the operator
makes no relation-actuality conclusion.

Evaluation precedence is fixed:

1. a grammar, type, arity, key-domain, or binding violation returns external `ILL_TYPED`;
2. `NOT_APPLICABLE` or unresolved application requires `NOT_EVALUATED`;
3. a substantive positive result is legal only where every premise required by the exact operator and Profile is established;
4. an established failed required premise prevents the positive result;
5. where no required premise is established false but at least one is unresolved, the substantive result remains unresolved;
6. a negative result requires an exact failed condition plus coverage adequate to that negative Claim; and
7. no aggregate token may contradict a constituent map or nested result.

Every finite map or inventory used substantively declares its exact finite key
domain, uses unique keys, requires each embedded item ID to equal its map key,
keeps every internal reference inside the host domain, rejects omitted, extra,
duplicate, foreign, and contradictory entries, keeps inventory closure distinct
from evidence coverage, and derives every aggregate deterministically from the
closed evaluated map. A violation is `ILL_TYPED`.

### CT0.9 Result and Error Discipline

The following are distinct:

- the continuity relation's actuality;
- the endpoints' actuality and realization;
- the dynamic bridge's actuality and coverage;
- the Criterion's applicability;
- strict identity posture;
- continuation under the Criterion;
- retained, transformed, reconstructed, duplicated, lost, and unresolved items;
- lineage and provenance;
- beginning and ending;
- the truth of a continuity Claim;
- the authenticity and persistence of a continuity Record;
- conformance to a Continuity Profile.

No result token may migrate between these dimensions.

Within any substantive family that also contains an explicit unresolved token,
`X_NOT_ESTABLISHED` or `NO_X_ESTABLISHED` means an evidence-supported negative
under adequate Scope and coverage. It never means merely that proof of X was not
found. Mere failure to decide is the family's unresolved posture.

Every substantive Continuity result family has one explicit unresolved member. `ILL_TYPED` means the invocation, schema, relation arity, qualifier, target Kind, or Scope violates the grammar. It is not evidence that continuity fails. `NOT_EVALUATED` means procedure did not reach substantive judgment. It is not `UNRESOLVED`. `NOT_APPLICABLE` belongs to application or a named dimension; it is never a silent negative.

Negative continuation requires evidence adequate to the exact Criterion, Interval, and coverage. Missing evidence, damaged Records, inaccessible endpoints, or an open observational gap produce unresolved posture unless the Profile's negative condition is itself adequately established.

`OPEN` and unresolved values in any outcome-material applicability, invariant,
transformation, loss, bridge, gap, breaking, ending, composition, or evidence
field are never interpreted as `NONE`, a wildcard, Permission, or satisfaction.
They prevent an established positive or negative substantive result unless an
already-applicable rule independently establishes that the field is nonmaterial
to that evaluation branch.

### CT0.10 Universal Continuity Invariants

1. `SAME` retains Ontology's strict, non-criterion-relative meaning.
2. `CONTINUES_AS` never entails or redefines `SAME`.
3. Independently established `SAME` does not force continuation under every Criterion.
4. One bearer may undergo extensive State, Configuration, component, and activity change.
5. Identical State or Configuration descriptions do not establish one bearer.
6. A new Copy is numerically distinct from its source.
7. Source ending after copying does not turn the Copy into the source.
8. Numerical identity cannot bifurcate across distinct branches.
9. A merge result cannot be numerically identical to multiple mutually distinct sources.
10. Branching and merging may establish plural criterion-relative continuities without fusing identity.
11. Lineage, provenance, continuity, strict identity, History, and Records remain separate.
12. A provenance, lineage, or continuity Record is not the actual relation or History it represents.
13. Persistence is not availability, activity, observability, accessibility, consensus, finality, or uninterrupted execution.
14. Suspension does not by itself establish dormancy; resumption does not establish same-Process continuity.
15. Restoring a prior Configuration does not restore the earlier historical State occurrence or erase intervening History.
16. Component replacement does not by itself preserve or destroy whole-System continuity.
17. Destruction followed by reconstruction is not strict persistence, although a successor or formal Continuity Criterion may establish a different continuation relation.
18. Discontinuity under one Criterion is not universal nonexistence and does not erase derivation or History.
19. `CONTINUES_AS` is not universally transitive; composition requires compatible Criteria, mappings, intervals, loss rules, and coverage.
20. Missing evidence yields unresolved placement, not presumed persistence or break.
21. Origin, source, predecessor, and founder relations create no Authority, Ownership, control, entitlement, permanent privilege, or value.
22. Constitutional succession and Carry may be inputs but do not prove descriptive continuity or transfer unstated interior State.
23. Descriptive continuity creates no constitutional Right, Obligation, Consent, Admission, Currentness, jurisdiction, or Effect.
24. Continuity does not establish life, Self, Subject status, Agency, Experience, intelligence, meaning, coherence, contribution, or value.
25. Lineage topology or density may be described, but its significance belongs to Emergence.
26. Human, biological, machine, derivative, institutional, artifact, and mixed bearers use the same root grammar; domain-specific differences require explicit Profiles.
27. Every Ending or discontinuity preserves the actuality and attributable history of what preceded it.
28. Failed, refused, partial, invalid, and nonconforming Occurrences may remain historically and continuously relevant; success is not a universal emission condition.
29. Append-only recording is a Profile property, not a metaphysical definition of continuity.
30. An unfamiliar case remains expressible through `CONTINUITY_PLACEMENT` before its continuity kind is understood.

## CT1 — Continuity Forms and Realization

### CT1.1 Actual Relation, Question, and Representation

An **actual Continuity Relation** is an obtaining `CONTINUES_AS`, `REPLACES`, `SUCCEEDS`, or other registered Continuity-owned Relation Instance among actual relata in a Scope.

A **Continuity Candidate** packages a continuity question. It does not answer it.

A **Continuity Criterion** states the exact rule under which selected persistence or continuation is assessed. It is a Representation, not the relation and not necessarily a constitutional Rule or physical Law.

A **Continuity Profile** is a versioned Representation organizing one or more compatible Continuity Criteria, endpoint constraints, bridge requirements, preservation and loss rules, gap treatment, composition rules, and evidence requirements for a bounded family of continuity questions.

A **Continuity Description** is a Representation claiming or describing continuity-related facts. A **Continuity Record** is a persistent Continuity Description or assessment Representation intended for recovery. A **Continuity Graph** is a Representation of selected nodes and edges. None is the actual relation, bearer, History, lineage, or provenance it represents.

### CT1.7 Coverage and Negative Claims

Continuity uses imported `CoveragePosture`. A negative conclusion requires coverage adequate to exclude the exact relation under the exact Criterion. Closed coverage of stored Records is not automatically closed coverage of physical or historical Reality.

Examples:

- no predecessor pointer in a database is not proof of no predecessor;
- no observed activity is not proof of Ending;
- no accessible source is not proof that the source ceased;
- no matching identifier is not proof of `DIFFERENT`;
- no differing State field is not proof of `SAME`;
- no surviving evidence can make a present assessment unresolved while the historical relation still actually obtained.

### CT1.8 Profile Composition and Versioning

Two Continuity Profiles compose only where:

1. their target families and endpoint constraints are compatible;
2. their Scopes overlap as declared;
3. their Criteria are equal or connected by an explicit non-lossy or loss-accounted mapping;
4. their required invariants, permitted transformations, and breaking conditions do not conflict, or conflict treatment is explicit;
5. their evidence and coverage requirements can be jointly satisfied;
6. composition does not smuggle in strict identity, constitutional force, or emergent value.

A version change creates a new Profile Representation. It does not retroactively change which Criterion governed a historical assessment. Re-evaluation under a later version is a new judgment about the same or a separately represented historical case; the earlier judgment remains part of History.

### CT1.9 Profile-Specific Vocabulary

A downstream domain Profile may declare qualified vocabulary for biological organisms, documents, software, networks, ledgers, institutions, accounts, models, Agents, Subjects, or other target families. Each term must declare its parent Kind, realization conditions, Scope, relation signature, evidence conditions, and non-entailments.

Project-specific terms such as `Continuity Turn`, turn emission, kernel persistence, chain succession, canonical branch, account recovery, or subject transfer are not universal Continuity primitives. They may be defined only in a downstream Profile that preserves this layer's identity, actuality, constitutional, and emergence firewalls.

## CT2 — Persistence Through Change

### CT2.1 Continuity Is a Relation, Not a Substance

Continuity is not a fluid, force, essence, energy, soul, token, balance, reward, or object carried invisibly from one endpoint to another. It is a typed Relation that may obtain between actual positions under an exact Criterion.

The Process that maintains, copies, repairs, replaces, transports, reconstructs, or transforms a bearer is not the Continuity Relation. It may provide the bridge history on which the relation is assessed.

### CT2.2 Persistence

**Persistence** is the continued existence of one bearer across an Interval despite some admitted change or variation.

Persistence requires:

- an actual bearer occupying the relevant earlier and later positions;
- an Interval and Resolution at which persistence is claimed;
- independently adequate identity placement where strict one-bearer persistence is asserted;
- an actual or adequately bounded bridge history;
- survival of every invariant required by the applicable persistence Criterion;
- no established disqualifying Ending or break under that Criterion.

Persistence is not:

- unchanging State;
- uninterrupted activity;
- permanent realization by the same components;
- observation at every instant;
- recoverability of every historical fact;
- the survival of a name, record, account, role, address, or representation;
- universal continuation under every possible Criterion.

One bearer can persist while its Properties, components, Configuration, location, activity, roles, relations, representations, or accessible memory change. Whether the permitted changes are compatible with persistence depends on the exact bearer Kind and Criterion; the word “persistence” does not supply that criterion by itself.

`CONTINUES_AS_ESTABLISHED` requires an actual registered Continuity Relation or an assessment basis sufficient to establish its obtaining, not merely satisfaction of a syntactic Profile. Its strict identity field is an independent projection. It is never derived from the Criterion satisfaction or continuation fields.

No Profile may waive the minimum ground for that result.
`CONTINUES_AS_ESTABLISHED` requires either:

1. independently established strict persistence of one bearer; or
2. an established directed source-to-receiver dependency plus at least one
   source-conditioned selected respect that is retained, mapped, transformed,
   or reconstructed across that dependency.

Temporal order, similarity, equivalence, a common Kind, label, name, identifier,
or `PRESENT_FROM_INDEPENDENT_SOURCE` cannot satisfy this floor. An observational
gap supports positive inference only where evidence adequate to the Profile
excludes independent formation; otherwise the result remains unresolved.
Succession or lineage may still be established separately without
`CONTINUES_AS`.

`CONTINUES_AS_ESTABLISHED` additionally requires
`CONTINUITY_CRITERION_SATISFIED`, an exact `RelationInstance` in
`established_relation_reference`, `RELATION_ACTUALITY_ESTABLISHED` in the
envelope binding, established actuality for every required endpoint, and either
`BRIDGE_HISTORY_ESTABLISHED` or one Profile-permitted observational gap with
adequate coverage. It is illegal with criterion non-satisfaction, relation
nonactuality, disproved bridge history, an unresolved required premise, or
`established_relation_reference = NONE`.

`DOES_NOT_CONTINUE_AS_ESTABLISHED` requires
`CONTINUITY_CRITERION_NOT_SATISFIED` or another exact disqualifying condition
established with adequate negative coverage. For that exact Criterion and
Scope, its relation-actuality binding is negative rather than established.
Where no disqualifying condition is established and any required premise is
unresolved, the only substantive continuation result is
`CONTINUITY_UNRESOLVED`.

For the edge-atomic central assessment, source and receiver endpoint maps and the
`endpoint_temporal_postures` map must contain exactly those two endpoint IDs.
Every topology operator must provide a total per-edge evaluation map and a total
temporal map over its declared endpoint set.

`DOES_NOT_CONTINUE_AS_ESTABLISHED` requires an applicable Criterion, adequate endpoint actuality, sufficient bridge or negative-history coverage, and an established failure of one or more required conditions or an established disqualifying break. It means only “does not continue under this Criterion in this Scope.”

### CT2.5 Continuity Assessment

    CONTINUITY_ASSESSMENT(candidate_k, profile_p, CS)
      -> ContinuationEvaluation | ILL_TYPED

The evaluation order is:

1. type the Candidate, Profile, and Scope;
2. evaluate application without using the desired result;
3. establish the actuality and required realization of each endpoint;
4. place the bridge history and its coverage;
5. evaluate each required invariant, permitted transformation, admitted loss, prohibited loss, gap, and breaking condition;
6. import but do not derive strict identity posture;
7. evaluate retention, lineage, provenance, source, and beginning/ending dimensions;
8. assert a Continuation Posture;
9. create a Record only as a Representation of the judgment where a recording Profile requires it.

The assessment may establish different results under different Criteria without contradiction. A contradiction exists only where target aspect, endpoints, Criterion and version, Scope, Interval, Resolution, interpretation, and evidence posture are the same.

### CT2.7 State Continuity and Bearer Continuity

A State is selected information about a bearer or system at a position. Similar or equal States can belong to distinct bearers. One bearer can persist across unequal States.

Therefore:

    equal selected State values -/-> SAME bearer
    different selected State values -/-> DIFFERENT bearer
    restored State values -/-> restored historical State occurrence
    State continuity -/-> bearer persistence
    bearer persistence -/-> invariant State

A State-continuity Profile must declare the selected variables, mapping, tolerance, permitted loss, ordering, and Resolution. It establishes only continuity of that selected State content.

### CT2.8 System and Organization Continuity

A System may continue under a Profile while components, operators, Processes, locations, or material constituents change. This requires an exact organization or System Criterion, not the mere reuse of a System name.

System continuity must distinguish:

- the System bearer, if independently established;
- its component and relation Configuration at each endpoint;
- which organizing relations must persist, recur, or be reconstructed;
- whether component replacement is permitted;
- whether operation may pause;
- whether distributed failover preserves the System while a component Process ends;
- whether a newly realized arrangement is a successor rather than the same persistent System.

A newly formed System has a Beginning, not an invented prehistory. Shared ancestry or inherited State may support `NEW_FORMATION_WITH_ANCESTRY` without establishing persistence.

### CT2.9 Process Continuity

Process continuity is not output equality or repeated invocation. It concerns whether one Process persists through stages, suspension, migration, or resumption under a Process-specific Criterion.

The following do not alone establish one continuing Process:

- the same request identifier;
- retry after failure;
- replay of the same inputs;
- re-execution of the same Program;
- identical outputs;
- restoration of a checkpoint into a new runtime;
- a successor Process occupying the same role;
- one Session label spanning multiple actual Sessions.

Dynamics owns whether the relevant execution, suspension, resumption, timeout, or restart Occurrences happened. Continuity classifies their cross-occurrence relation.

### CT2.10 Relation and Relational-System Continuity

A Relation is distinct from its relata. A continuing Relation must be assessed at the relation position itself. Persistence of all participants does not guarantee persistence of the Relation; replacement of one participant does not automatically end it.

For a Relational System, the Profile must state:

- which Relation Instances and organization are constitutive;
- which participant changes are admitted;
- what State-bearing organization must remain;
- what gaps or reconstructions are permitted;
- whether a new relation after ending counts as restoration, succession, or new formation;
- how relation-local History is distinguished from participant histories.

This supplies the persistence/history basis that Structure intentionally leaves open. It does not revise the prior structural Configuration or create a Relational System by assessment.

### CT2.11 Formal and Realization Continuity

A Form or Pattern may be preserved across distinct physical realizations. A physical realization may continue while the represented Form changes. These are different target families.

Formal continuity requires an explicit mapping of the selected formal structure. Realization continuity concerns actual realizers and their persistence. Neither implies the other, and neither implies bearer identity across distinct realizations.

### CT2.12 Observation Gaps

An observation gap creates an epistemic limit, not automatically an ontological break.

Where a Profile permits inference across a gap, it must state:

- the maximum or kind of gap admitted;
- the retained traces or boundary conditions required;
- alternative histories that have been excluded;
- the confidence or epistemic posture;
- whether the result is persistence, criterion-relative continuation, or only unresolved placement.

Unknown gaps return `PERSISTENCE_UNRESOLVED` or `CONTINUITY_UNRESOLVED` unless adequate evidence supports a stronger result.

## CT3 — Identity and Equivalence Boundary

### CT3.1 Strict Numerical Identity Is Imported

`SAME(x, y)` means that x and y are one and the same bearer. It remains reflexive, symmetric, and transitive as declared by Ontology. It does not vary with purpose, convenience, protocol, institutional rule, or comparison criterion.

Continuity does not define the metaphysical criterion of strict identity for every Kind. It can evaluate and preserve attributable identity Claims and judgments without deriving them from its own continuation result.

If a rule says two distinct bearers “count as the same” for a purpose, the relation is qualified Equivalence, substitution, role continuity, legal or institutional treatment, or criterion-relative Continuity—not strict `SAME`.

`SAME_ESTABLISHED` and `DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED` require an independently admissible Ontology-level judgment basis for the exact bearer Kind. A Continuity Criterion may specify what evidence it accepts, but it cannot make numerical identity relative to itself. Where neither result is adequately established, the result is `IDENTITY_UNRESOLVED`.

### CT3.3 Qualitative Identity, Similarity, and Equivalence

The following remain distinct:

| Relation | Question | Does it entail `SAME`? |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical identity | Is this one and the same bearer? | It is `SAME` |
| Qualitative identity | Do selected Properties exactly match? | No |
| Similarity | Do selected Properties partly correspond? | No |
| `EQUIVALENT_UNDER` | Are the bearers interchangeable under an exact criterion? | No |
| `CONTINUES_AS` | Does the source continue as the receiver under an exact Continuity Criterion? | No |
| `DERIVED_FROM` | Is the receiver derived from a source under a lineage type? | No |
| `SUCCEEDS` | Does a descriptive predecessor/successor relation obtain? | No |
| `REPLACES` | Does one bearer displace another in a context? | No |

Two items may be bitwise equal, structurally isomorphic, behaviorally equivalent, functionally interchangeable, or legally treated alike while numerically distinct. One bearer may become qualitatively unlike its earlier State while remaining numerically one bearer.

### CT3.4 Names, Identifiers, Addresses, Accounts, Keys, and Hashes

Names and identifiers support reference within conventions. Addresses support location or routing. Accounts group selected State and operations. Keys support cryptographic operations or control Claims. Hashes support comparison of encoded content. None is the bearer.

Therefore:

- reuse of an identifier by a later bearer does not continue the earlier bearer;
- change of identifier does not end the bearer;
- one account may represent successive Agents;
- one Agent may use several accounts;
- key rotation need not break account or System continuity;
- possession of the same key does not prove the same Agent;
- equal hashes in two Worlds do not establish one cross-World bearer;
- a filename or version label can remain while content and bearer change;
- a persistent Profile may represent a nonpersistent or changing target.

Identity Claims based on these signs require an explicit maintained reference relation and additional evidence appropriate to the bearer Kind.

### CT3.5 Role and Status Continuity

A Role or Status may continue while its bearer changes. A bearer may continue while entering or leaving a Role or Status.

Role-continuity assessment concerns the structured Role position and its History. Bearer-continuity assessment concerns the occupant. Institutional Status continuity concerns a constituted Status and is constrained by Constitution. None can be substituted for another.

    role persists -/-> occupant persists
    occupant persists -/-> role persists
    same role name -/-> same role instance
    role succession -/-> SAME bearer

### CT3.6 Copy, Branch, and Merge Identity Limits

A Copy is a new realization and therefore numerically distinct from its source. If a source and Copy coexist, their distinction is direct. Later source Ending does not retroactively remove that distinction.

At a branch, distinct receivers cannot each be numerically identical to the one source. Each may have a separate `CONTINUES_AS` or `DERIVED_FROM` relation under a suitable Criterion.

At a merge, one receiver cannot be numerically identical to multiple mutually distinct sources. It may continue selected organization, State, Form, or lineage from several sources while remaining a distinct bearer.

### CT3.7 Agent and Subject Continuity Boundary

Repeated model weights, a shared account, retained memory records, conversational resemblance, biometric similarity, brain-state copying, narrative self-identification, or social recognition does not alone settle Agent or Subject continuity.

Continuity may evaluate a qualified Agent-continuity or Subject-continuity
Candidate only after the independent Emergence placement required by CT1.3 has
established the relevant target Kind. No alternative source bypasses that staged
route. Continuity may not bootstrap Agency, Experience, consciousness, or
Subject status from continuity language.

A digital twin, brain scan, clone, model replica, or restored checkpoint may establish copying, derivation, similarity, or functional continuation. Subjective or experiential continuity remains unresolved unless its sufficient conditions are separately available and satisfied.

### CT3.8 Cross-World Identity and Mapping

A cross-World mapping relates positions in distinct World Contexts. It does not make their occupants one bearer. Cross-World `SAME` requires an independently justified identity relation capable of spanning those World Contexts. Otherwise the proper results are equivalence, representation, derivation, continuation, or unresolved placement.

### CT3.9 Identity Firewall Failures

An assessment is `ILL_TYPED` or `DOES_NOT_CONFORM` as appropriate if it:

- defines `SAME` by a Continuity Criterion;
- derives `SAME` from `CONTINUES_AS`, `EQUIVALENT_UNDER`, Copy, Lineage, or `SUCCEEDS`;
- permits numerical identity to branch or fuse;
- treats a sign or Record as the bearer by default;
- treats legal or institutional sameness as metaphysical identity;
- makes source Ending transfer numerical identity;
- makes success, consensus, or finality prove identity.

## CT4 — Retention, Transformation, and Loss

### CT4.1 Why Retention Must Be Explicit

“Continuity” without an item-level account can hide what was preserved, changed, reconstructed, duplicated, lost, or left unresolved. This module provides that account without importing Constitution's normative `Carry` operator.

The universal term is **retention**. Constitutional Carry remains a separate institutional treatment determined only by Constitution.

### CT4.2 Continuity Requirement

`ContinuityRequirement` and `RequirementStrength` are declared in CT1 because they are constituent parts of a Continuity Criterion. CT4 evaluates them item by item. Requirements are declared before substantive assessment or versioned as changed Criteria. A result-dependent requirement silently chosen after the fact cannot support the earlier Criterion.

### CT4.7 Transformation and Mapping

Retention need not mean byte-for-byte or constituent-for-constituent preservation. A Criterion may admit a declared mapping or transformation. The mapping must state:

- its source and receiver Domains;
- the selected structure or Property preserved;
- information or distinction loss;
- invertibility, if any;
- error or tolerance bounds;
- realization and execution basis;
- provenance and evidence;
- composition limits.

A lossy mapping may support continuity where the Criterion permits that exact loss. It cannot be described as lossless. A reversible formal mapping does not make the physical Process reversible or the bearers identical.

### CT4.8 Reconstruction

Reconstruction realizes receiver-side content from retained traces, rules, components, or records. It differs from continuous preservation.

`RECONSTRUCTED_FROM_RETAINED_BASIS` requires:

- an actual reconstruction Process;
- identified retained basis;
- an exact mapping from basis to receiver content;
- receiver realization;
- explicit differences and uncertainty;
- a Criterion that permits reconstruction.

Reconstruction may support successor, formal, functional, or recovery continuity. It does not establish strict persistence through a period in which the bearer did not exist.

### CT4.9 Duplication

Duplication preserves selected source content in an additional receiver while the source content or bearer may remain. It creates no numerical identity and no exclusive transfer.

`DUPLICATED_TO_RECEIVER` must not be used where the receiver independently possessed equivalent content. Source and receiver histories remain distinct after duplication even if their selected States initially match.

### CT4.10 Partial Continuity

Continuity can be established under one selected respect and fail under another. “Partial continuity” is not a vague midpoint token. It is a set of independently typed results, each with its own Criterion, target aspect, and Scope.

For example:

- formal structure may continue while carrier continuity fails;
- service organization may continue while Process continuity fails;
- role continuity may continue while bearer continuity fails;
- lineage may continue while required State retention fails;
- account State may continue while Agent continuity is unresolved.

No aggregate label may hide those dimensions.

### CT4.11 No Silent Retention

No property, State field, authority, obligation, memory, identity, consent, ownership, debt, balance, right, restriction, or privilege crosses a continuity relation by silence.

Descriptive retention must be established item by item under this module. Normative transfer or constitutional Carry must be separately established by Constitution. A system that copied bytes may still lack any lawful basis to treat the copied rights or permissions as transferred.

Where an inventory item names Constitution-owned Authority, Right, Obligation,
Consent, Permission, Binding, Responsibility, Currentness, institutional Status,
or Effect, Continuity may assess retention of the Claim, Record, or encoded bytes
without constitutional input. Retention of the normative Relation or Status
itself requires an exact applicable `ConstitutionalJudgmentReference`, including
`CARRIES` where transfer is claimed. Without it, the normative dimension is
`REFERENCE_NOT_EVALUATED` or unresolved and may never receive a `RETAINED_*`
disposition merely from copied content.

## CT5 — Repair, Replacement, and Restoration

### CT5.1 Distinct Change Families

The following actual Processes and relations are distinct:

- **Repair** modifies or replaces something to restore or improve selected functioning, integrity, or conformance.
- **Replacement** installs one bearer in a position, role, or whole where another bearer was displaced, removed, failed, ended, or ceased to occupy it.
- **Restoration** re-establishes selected prior conditions or relations.
- **Reconstruction** realizes selected content or organization from retained bases after loss, absence, or destruction.
- **Rollback** is a dynamic attempt to restore selected earlier State values.
- **Remediation** addresses an established defect, harm, violation, or undesired condition under a governing criterion.
- **Regeneration** is a domain-qualified Process producing or reconstituting parts or organization through an actual mechanism.

None names a continuity result by itself. Dynamics owns the actual change. Continuity owns whether and how the affected bearer, whole, relation, process, or Form continues across it.

### CT5.2 Replacement Relation

`REPLACES(replacement, displaced; replacement_context)` obtains only where an actual context has a typed position or function from which the displaced bearer ceases or is reduced and which the replacement bearer occupies or assumes under the declared structural and dynamic conditions.

Replacement does not entail:

- that the displaced bearer ended;
- that the replacement is its successor;
- that either bearer is the same as the other;
- that the whole persists;
- that all State, function, rights, obligations, authority, memory, or value transfer;
- that the replacement is adequate or conforming.

A role occupant may be replaced while the role continues. A component may be replaced while the whole continues. A whole may be replaced by a new whole. These require separate target aspects and results.

### CT5.3 Part Replacement and Whole Continuity

The classic replacement pressure is not resolved by counting parts. A whole-continuity Profile must declare:

- the Whole or System target;
- the relevant part inventory or organization;
- which parts or relations are constitutive;
- whether replacement may be simultaneous, sequential, recurrent, or total over time;
- which organizing relations must persist;
- what material, formal, functional, causal, historical, or provenance continuity is required;
- whether replaced parts later reassembled form a new whole, a successor, or another unresolved case;
- permitted interruption and reconstruction;
- breaking conditions.

No fixed replacement percentage universally decides identity or continuity. A Profile may use quantitative thresholds only where it declares their semantic and empirical basis.

The per-aspect map has exactly the `target_aspects` domain. A missing, extra, or

### CT5.5 Repair Continuity

Repair may:

- preserve one bearer through modification;
- replace one or more components while the whole persists;
- create a successor resembling the prior bearer;
- fail while leaving the earlier bearer changed or damaged;
- restore function without restoring form or history;
- restore conformance under one Profile while breaking continuity under another.

A Repair Record is Evidence of a repair Claim. It does not prove successful repair or continuity. The repairer's identity, authority, intention, or ownership is not inferred from performing the repair.

### CT5.6 Restoration

Restoration is always restoration **of selected conditions under an exact mapping**. It is not reversal of History.

    restored selected values -/-> same historical State occurrence
    restored Configuration -/-> erased intervening change
    restored function -/-> restored bearer
    restored Record -/-> complete recovered History
    restored constitutional text -/-> restored prior Currentness

A restoration Profile must state the target conditions, reference boundary, mapping, admitted approximation, missing distinctions, actual restoration Process, receiver bearer, and whether the relation sought is persistence, recovery continuity, successor continuity, or equivalence.

### CT5.7 Reconstruction After Destruction

Where adequate evidence establishes that a bearer ended and a later bearer was reconstructed, strict persistence through the absence is not established. The later bearer may be:

- a Copy;
- a new Realization Instance;
- a successor;
- a restored formal or functional continuation;
- a new formation with ancestry;
- an unresolved target.

The applicable Criterion decides which continuation relations obtain. No Criterion may relabel a period of established nonexistence as continuous strict persistence.

### CT5.8 Failover and Redundancy

In a distributed System, an active component or Process may fail and another may take over. System continuity can be established where the distributed System was already the target bearer and its required organization persists. This does not make the replacement Process the same Process or the replacement component the same component.

If no prior distributed System bearer was established, failover language cannot invent one retroactively. The case may instead be replacement, succession, or new formation with ancestry.

### CT5.9 Repair and Replacement Records

Every repair, replacement, restoration, or reconstruction Description must identify:

- the actual target and target aspect;
- prior and subsequent endpoint references;
- the actual dynamic Process or Occurrence;
- replaced, modified, retained, and lost items;
- the Criterion and version;
- strict identity posture where material;
- source and receiver postures;
- evidence coverage;
- unresolved positions;
- constitutional judgments only as separately typed inputs.

## CT6 — Copying, Replication, Migration, and Reinstantiation

### CT6.1 Copy

Ontology defines a Copy as a new realization produced to preserve selected structure from a source. This entails a new realization and therefore a numerically distinct bearer from the source.

A Copy relation requires:

- an actual source position;
- an actual copy-production Process or adequate derivation history;
- an actual receiver realization;
- an explicit selected structure and mapping;
- preservation, transformation, and loss results;
- provenance adequate to establish derivation rather than independent similarity.

Bitwise equality, isomorphism, output equality, shared weights, matched memory, or a copied identifier can support selected equivalence Claims. None turns the Copy into its source.

### CT6.4 Replication

Replication is a Process producing or maintaining multiple related realizations. It may produce Copies, synchronized replicas, redundant components, or only repeated outputs. Each result requires its own bearer, realization, derivation, and retention placement.

Replicas may diverge after a shared source State. Synchronization does not fuse them. A replica set may itself realize a distributed System whose continuity differs from that of each replica.

  a separately evaluated System-continuity reference;
- an unresolved Migration Mode cannot carry an established mode-specific
  identity conclusion.

### CT6.6 Checkpoint, Snapshot, and Restore

A checkpoint or snapshot is a Representation or copied State basis. It is not the running Process, complete System, Agent, Subject, or full History.

Restoring a checkpoint can produce:

- a later State of the same persistent bearer;
- a new Process within the same System;
- a new Realization Instance;
- a Copy or branch;
- a successor;
- an unresolved case.

Two restore Occurrences do not by themselves establish two bearer realizations:
they may successively modify one independently established bearer. Where the
Occurrences realize separately begun or coexisting receivers, those receivers
are distinct. Equal checkpoint basis or initial State never decides that issue.

### CT6.7 Reinstantiation

**Reinstantiation** is realization of a Form, Pattern, Program, or selected State after an earlier realization ended or elsewhere. It preserves `INSTANCE_OF` or formal derivation where established. It does not establish strict persistence of the earlier Realization Instance.

A reinstantiation may support formal, service, role, or successor continuity under a Profile. It begins a new realization interval.

### CT6.8 Source Ending and Copy Status

Source persistence and Copy realization are independent dimensions:

| Source posture | Receiver posture | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Source persists | Copy realized | Coexisting distinct bearers |
| Source ends after Copy | Copy realized | Distinct receiver with source lineage; no identity transfer |
| Source ends before receiver | Receiver reconstructed from traces | Reconstruction or succession; not strict persistence through absence |
| Source posture unresolved | Receiver realized | Copy/lineage may be established while source Ending remains unresolved |
| Receiver not realized | Source persists or ends | Attempted copy only; no actual Copy bearer |

### CT6.9 Software, Models, and Accounts

Two software Processes executing the same Program are distinct Processes. Two
model instances with identical weights are distinct realization instances. Two
accounts with identical State are distinct accounts where their system positions
are distinct. An account restored from backup may `CONTINUE_AS` under a qualified
account Criterion; `SAME` requires an independent account-identity basis and is
never made criterion-relative. The restored runtime Process may still be new.

No such result settles Agent or Subject continuity without the relevant downstream target classification and Profile.

## CT7 — Branching, Forking, and Divergence

### CT7.1 Branching

**Branching** is a one-to-many topology in which two or more post-boundary
receiver histories extend or derive from a shared source history or endpoint
through one or more actual branch-producing Occurrences or Processes. One
receiver may be the persisting source path; every other receiver is a derived
branch path.

A **fork** is a domain-qualified branching Process or result. The term alone does not say whether the source persists, whether receivers are Copies, whether one branch is canonical, or whether any bearer continues.

### CT7.2 Branch Invariants

1. Each receiver is separately actual and realized where required.
2. Every edge is typed as a persisting-source path or a derived-branch path.
3. A shared prefix is not a shared continuing numerical identity after distinct receivers exist.
4. Strict numerical identity does not bifurcate.
5. Each receiver's `CONTINUES_AS` result is evaluated separately.
6. The source may persist, end, transform, or remain unresolved.
7. Divergence does not erase the shared prefix.
8. Later selection of one preferred or canonical branch does not make other actual branches never have existed.
9. Branch count, depth, activity, adoption, or survival does not create value or Authority.

### CT7.3 Shared Prefix and Divergence Boundary

      receiver_endpoint_reference

A shared prefix is a historical and lineage relation. It does not make later receivers the same bearer. The divergence boundary may be unresolved even when distinct receivers are established.

receiver map, per-edge Continuation evaluations, and pairwise-distinction map
are total over the exact receiver set. Each receiver result's endpoint ID equals
its map key; its edge ID names the unique extent edge from the source to that
receiver; and its source posture is evaluated relative to that receiver. For
`n` receivers, the pair map contains exactly `n(n-1)/2` canonical unordered
pairs, each inner pair key equals its map key, and each identity projection is
for those two exact receiver bearers. The temporal map domain is the source ID
union the receiver-map domain. A missing, extra, duplicate, or foreign key is
`ILL_TYPED`.

The per-receiver lineage map has exactly the receiver-map domain and equals the
posture projected by each receiver's bound lineage result. The aggregate is
`BRANCH_LINEAGE_ESTABLISHED` where at least one receiver has established direct
or ancestral lineage, `BRANCH_LINEAGE_NOT_ESTABLISHED` only under adequate
closed negative coverage for every receiver, and
`BRANCH_LINEAGE_UNRESOLVED` otherwise.

A `PERSISTING_SOURCE_PATH` requires independently established source persistence
and `SAME_ESTABLISHED`; its direct-derivation posture is
`DIRECT_DERIVATION_NOT_APPLICABLE`. A `DERIVED_BRANCH_PATH` requires direct
derivation and `DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED`. At most one receiver may be the persisting
source path. All established receiver bearers must be pairwise `DIFFERENT`; if
receiver distinction is inadequate, `BRANCHING_ESTABLISHED` is unavailable.
`BRANCHING_ESTABLISHED` additionally requires every canonical receiver-pair
projection to be `DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED`, grounded by its exact Ontology
judgment references, and every branch edge kind to be exactly
`PERSISTING_SOURCE_PATH` or `DERIVED_BRANCH_PATH`. An unresolved or
not-applicable edge kind cannot support established branching.
This permits a mainline plus derived forks without self-derivation and never
permits numerical identity to bifurcate.

### CT7.5 Source and Branch Postures

A branch Profile must independently locate:

- whether the source persists alongside branches;
- whether source operation stops without source Ending;
- whether one receiver replaces the source in a role;
- whether source State was copied or transformed;
- whether any receiver is a successor;
- whether the source itself was only a historical endpoint rather than a persisting bearer.

“The branch became the source” is not a valid identity result. The proper relation may be replacement, succession, continuation, canonical selection, or unresolved.

### CT7.6 Canonical Branch Selection

Canonicality is a Status assigned under a protocol, institutional, or evaluative criterion. Dynamics may establish which branch a protocol currently extends. Constitution may govern valid assignment where normative effect is claimed. Continuity records the branch lineage and selected Status separately.

An orphaned, rejected, losing, or noncanonical branch may contain actual Occurrences and History. Loss of canonical status does not erase them.

### CT7.7 Nested Branching

Branches may branch again. Direct derivation and ancestral derivation remain distinct. A descendant of one branch is not automatically a direct child of the original source. The lineage graph must preserve intermediate nodes unless the relation is explicitly ancestral and its path coverage is declared.

## CT8 — Merging, Reconciliation, and Convergence

### CT8.1 Merge

**Merging** is a many-to-one lineage topology in which selected content, organization, relations, or histories from two or more source endpoints contribute through actual merge or reconciliation Processes to one receiver endpoint.

The receiver may be a new bearer, a later State of one source bearer, a continuation of a pre-existing distributed System, or an unresolved case. The word “merge” does not decide this.

### CT8.2 Reconciliation and Convergence

**Reconciliation** is an actual Process that compares or resolves selected differences under a rule or criterion. **Convergence** is increasing correspondence or arrival at a selected common condition. Either can occur without a Merge bearer, and a Merge may occur without complete convergence.

Dynamics owns comparison, conflict-handling, selection, combination, and commit Occurrences. Continuity owns how source histories and receiver relations stand across them.

### CT8.3 Merge Invariants

1. Each source endpoint is separately typed and actual where required.
2. The receiver is separately typed, actual, and realized.
3. Contribution or derivation is evaluated per source-to-receiver edge.
4. The receiver cannot be numerically identical to multiple mutually distinct sources.
5. Conflicts, omissions, overwrites, and unresolved content remain explicit.
6. Source histories are not retroactively fused into one past.
7. A receiver History may include multiple lineage paths without making their earlier Occurrences one event.
8. Source persistence or Ending is independent of merge success.
9. Consensus or canonical selection is not identical to Merge.
10. Merge success creates no Authority, Ownership, truth, or value by itself.

`SAME_AS_EXACTLY_ONE_SOURCE_ESTABLISHED` is permitted only where the receiver is independently established as the continuing State of one source bearer and the other sources contribute content or relations without becoming that bearer. contribution or direct derivation edge and cannot use self-derivation. The
source-reference and source-result maps have exactly the same key domain, and
each internal endpoint ID equals its map key. Each source result's edge ID names
the unique extent edge from that source to the receiver. Per-edge continuation
results are total over that source domain. For `n` sources, the pairwise-source
map contains exactly `n(n-1)/2` canonical unordered pairs; every inner pair key
equals its map key and every identity projection binds to those exact source

endpoint ID. Conflict-map keys equal their internal `conflict_id`. Any mismatch,
duplicate, omission, extra key, or unresolved edge kind in an established Merge
is `ILL_TYPED`.

lineage reference's result. `REQUIRED_CONFLICTS_CLOSED` requires a closed
conflict inventory, an actual treatment occurrence for every established
material conflict whose treatment is required, no `TREATMENT_UNRESOLVED`, and
an aggregate retention/loss projection consistent with every conflict entry.

`PERSISTING_BASE_SOURCE_PATH` or `DERIVED_CONTRIBUTOR_PATH`. An unresolved or
not-applicable edge kind cannot support established merging. It additionally
requires every canonical source-pair projection to be
`DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED`. Co-denoting source endpoints are collapsed to one
source before a many-to-one Merge is assessed; unresolved source-source
identity leaves the Merge unresolved.

`MERGE_ESTABLISHED` is unavailable while any required conflict, treatment,
contribution edge, receiver realization, or bridge actuality is open,
negative, or unresolved.

`DISTINCT_FROM_ALL_SOURCES_ESTABLISHED` requires every per-source identity
posture to be `DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED`.
`SAME_AS_EXACTLY_ONE_SOURCE_ESTABLISHED` requires exactly one
`PERSISTING_BASE_SOURCE_PATH` with `SAME_ESTABLISHED` and every other source to
be `DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED`. No aggregate token may contradict a per-source edge.

### CT8.6 Source Ending and Receiver Beginning

A Merge may:

- leave every source persisting;
- end one or more source operations while bearers persist;
- coincide with source Ending;
- modify one source bearer as receiver;
- realize a new receiver bearer;
- remain unresolved due to incomplete bridge history.

These postures are recorded per source. A single “merged” token is insufficient.

### CT8.7 Re-Merge and Cyclic Histories

Previously diverged branches may later merge. This creates a reticulate lineage graph, not a single linear ancestry. The earlier branch distinction remains historical. A lineage type may prohibit cycles in direct derivation, but graph acyclicity is not inferred from the word Lineage; it must be declared and evaluated under CT12–CT13.

### CT8.8 Merge Without Erasure

The receiver's selected State may become canonical, final, compacted, or authoritative under another layer or Profile. Continuity still preserves:

- source endpoint actuality;
- actual conflict and reconciliation Occurrences;
- source-to-receiver derivation paths;
- omitted or transformed distinctions;
- unresolved source contributions;
- source and receiver beginning/ending postures.

No compaction or finality rule can alter those historical facts by declaration.

## CT9 — Succession, Substitution, Revision, and Versioning

### CT9.1 Descriptive Succession

**Descriptive succession** is an actual ordered Relation in which one Bearer
follows another Bearer as successor under an exact succession kind and basis.
System, Form, institution, Role, Status, version, or organized-position
succession is expressed through actual bearer or `ReferenceTargetPosition`
endpoints plus the selected respect; those non-Bearer types are not silently cast
as relata.

Succession differs from:

- strict numerical identity;
- persistence of one bearer;
- Copy or derivation alone;
- replacement alone;
- temporal sequence alone;
- constitutional Succession;
- inheritance of every State or relation;
- Currentness or validity;
- control, Ownership, Authority, entitlement, or value.

A successor can be numerically distinct from its predecessor and may continue selected Form, function, institution, lineage, or organization. A persistent bearer undergoing change may have no successor at all.

### CT9.2 Succession Kinds

    SuccessionKind :=

A declaration may be Evidence of a succession Claim. It is not the succession
relation. `INSTITUTIONAL_SUCCESSION` requires

judgment establishes the relevant formation or succession Effect. Generic actual
formation, predecessor relation, declaration, ancestry, or technical continuity
can establish descriptive lineage or `NEW_FORMATION_WITH_ANCESTRY`, but no
institutional Effect. Descriptive physical, technical, System, Process, Agent,
or Subject continuities remain separate.

### CT9.4 New Formation With Ancestry

`NEW_FORMATION_WITH_ANCESTRY` is the honest posture where:

- a new bearer, System, network, document, or realization is established; an
  institution is established only where independently valid constitutional
  formation is supplied when that Kind requires it;
- one or more derivation or historical ancestry relations are established;
- the conditions for succession or persistence are not established, not applicable, or expressly absent.

This posture preserves lineage without inventing authority or sameness. It is not a lesser ontological status. A new formation can be real, functional, and historically related without being the successor under a claimed Profile.

projections, never bare Claims or Records and never computed from descriptive
succession. `SUCCESSION_ESTABLISHED` cannot supply missing constitutional
competence. An institutional succession result without the required resolved
constitutional reference is `ILL_TYPED` or not substantively evaluated.

`DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED` for successor and predecessor. Where

- `DIFFERENT_ESTABLISHED` for predecessor and new formation;
- `BEGINNING_ESTABLISHED` for the new formation;
- at least one resolved direct or ancestral Lineage assessment;
- no strict-persistence result for the new bearer across the predecessor's
  interval.

If any required position is unresolved, the new-formation posture remains
unresolved. The ancestry posture creates no institutional Effect or Authority.

### CT9.6 Substitution

**Substitution** is use or placement of one bearer, item, procedure, or Representation in place of another for a specified purpose. It may depend on equivalence, Permission, replacement, or operational availability. It does not entail succession, continuity, derivation, or identity.

Substitution can be temporary, partial, reversible, or context-limited. The displaced item may remain actual and available. A substitute inheriting a role does not inherit every relation of the prior occupant.

### CT9.7 Revision

A **Revision** is a later Form or Representation produced through an actual editing or transformation history from an earlier Form or Representation.

Revision requires:

- separately identified earlier and later Forms;
- actual derivation or editing history;
- declared changed, retained, added, and removed content;
- provenance;
- a revision relation under an exact Profile.

The revised Form is not numerically identical to the earlier byte sequence or physical Record. It may be a later State of one document bearer under a document Profile, or a distinct successor Form. The target aspect must be stated.

A correction of an error creates a later Representation or State; it does not alter what the earlier Form contained or what was previously done under it.

### CT9.8 Versioning

A version is an Identifier or Status within a versioning convention. Version order may represent temporal order, compatibility order, intended succession, or release order. It does not establish any of them outside the convention's actual basis.

    higher version number -/-> valid successor
    later timestamp -/-> Current Form
    compatible implementation -/-> same System
    same version label -/-> same bytes
    deprecation -/-> physical Ending

Version succession requires an actual relation and Profile, not lexicographic comparison alone.

### CT9.9 Form Succession and Currentness

Continuity may describe that Form B derives from, revises, or succeeds Form A. Constitution alone determines whether B validly amends, repeals, replaces, or becomes Current in a constitutional Scope.

Re-adoption of byte-identical earlier content creates a new institutional Interval or Act relation where validly constituted. It does not make the intervening constitutional History disappear or literally reverse time.

### CT9.10 No Self-Succession

A candidate cannot establish its succession by declaring itself successor, defining its predecessor as superseded, or treating its own implementation as proof. The required succession basis must be independent of the result and, where constitutional effect is claimed, independent of the candidate's own unactivated authority.

## CT10 — Dormancy, Interruption, Resumption, and Recovery

### CT10.1 Inactivity Is Not One State

The absence of observed activity may correspond to:

- ordinary inactivity;
- sleep or a biological phase;
- dynamic suspension;
- operational hold;
- unavailable resources;
- disconnected observation;
- dormant persistence;
- Process Ending while a bearer or System persists;
- bearer Ending;
- destroyed evidence;
- unknown conditions.

No one of these is inferred from silence alone.

### CT10.2 Dormancy

**Dormancy** is criterion-qualified persistence through an Interval of reduced, suspended, absent, or non-observed activity, supported by a retained organization or recovery basis sufficient under the applicable Profile.

Dormancy requires:

- a target bearer or System;
- an inactive or low-activity Interval;
- a Profile declaring what inactivity is admitted;
- a retained physical, structural, biological, computational, institutional, or other basis;
- no established disqualifying Ending;
- evidence coverage adequate to distinguish dormancy from known alternatives.

Dormancy is not mere potential, memory Record, backup availability, possibility of reconstruction, or cultural remembrance. The retained basis must concern the continuing target under the Profile.

### CT10.4 Interruption

An **interruption** is a bounded break in selected activity, relation, observation, or operation. Its continuity significance depends on the target and Criterion.

An activity interruption may be irrelevant to bearer persistence. A constitutive-relation interruption may end a Relational System under one Profile. An observation interruption may create only uncertainty. Every interruption Claim must identify what was interrupted.

### CT10.5 Resumption

Dynamics owns the actual resumption Occurrence. Continuity asks whether what resumes is:

- the same persistent Process;
- a new Process of the same System;
- a successor Process;
- a replay or retry;
- a reconstructed instance;
- an unresolved target.

The same request, Session, account, checkpoint, or operation identifier does not decide this.

### CT10.6 Recovery

**Recovery** is actual re-establishment of selected capacity, organization, State, relation, access, or operation after disruption, loss, failure, or unavailability. Recovery is dynamic in occurrence and continuous only in its cross-boundary relation.

Recovery does not entail:

- full restoration;
- same bearer;
- absence of loss;
- successful remediation of every consequence;
- erased failure History;
- validity, Review, or Remedy completion;
- resilience as an emergent capacity.

recovery occurrence cannot carry an established mode-specific recovery result.

evaluation for the exact source and recovered receiver. `RECOVERY_OCCURRED`
requires the actual recovery bridge binding; a Record or Claim alone cannot
establish it.

prior and recovered endpoint IDs. Each `EndpointTemporalPosture` carries the
same internal endpoint ID as its key. Missing, extra, duplicated, or foreign
keys are `ILL_TYPED`.

### CT10.8 Recovery Basis and Backup

A backup, archive, seed, genome, template, checkpoint, specification, or memory Record can be a recovery basis. Its existence does not mean the target bearer remained dormant or that recovery has occurred.

Recovery basis continuity, bearer continuity, State continuity, and realized recovery are separate. A backup may persist while the System ends; a System may recover without a complete backup; a restored backup may realize a new System.

### CT10.9 Human and Machine Cases

Human sleep and machine suspension share the same root grammar: actual target, inactive Interval, retained basis, no established disqualifying Ending, recovery or resumption Occurrence, and Criterion. They may require very different domain Profiles and evidence. The labels “human” and “machine” do not install different truth rules or predetermined continuity outcomes.

### CT10.10 Recovery Does Not Prove Resilience

One successful recovery is an actual history. Resilience is an Emergence-layer capacity requiring a criterion across perturbations, organization, and possible repeated behavior. Continuity passes recovery histories downstream without declaring resilience.

## CT11 — Ending, Dissolution, and Discontinuity

### CT11.1 Ending Is Target-Specific

An Ending is the bounded cessation of an Existent, Process, Relation, State,
role occupancy, Status, System organization, or another exact descriptive
target. Form Currentness and constitutional Effect ending remain
Constitution-owned. “It ended” is ill-typed until the target aspect and Scope
are named.

Distinct endings include:

    EndingTargetKind :=
        ACTIVITY_ENDING
      | PROCESS_ENDING
      | SESSION_ENDING
      | BEARER_ENDING
      | RELATION_ENDING
      | STATE_ENDING
      | RELATIONAL_SYSTEM_ENDING
      | SYSTEM_ORGANIZATION_ENDING
      | ROLE_OCCUPANCY_ENDING
      | STATUS_ENDING
      | REALIZATION_ENDING
      | CONTINUITY_RELATION_ENDING
      | OBSERVATION_ENDING
      | RECORD_RECOVERABILITY_ENDING
      | OTHER_REGISTERED_ENDING

One Ending does not imply the others. Process Ending can occur while the bearer
persists. A Relation can end while both relata persist. A System can dissolve
while components persist. Observation can end while its target continues.
Form Currentness and constitutional Effect ending are Constitution-owned
judgments, not members of this descriptive target family. Their exact results may
be imported through the separate projection below.

### CT11.2 Ending Occurrence and Ending Relation

Dynamics owns actual stopping, shutdown, destruction, separation, disconnection, disintegration, timeout, cancellation, or cessation Occurrences. Continuity evaluates which exact target ceases and which histories or relations remain.

An Ending Record represents an Ending Claim. It does not end the target unless a separately realized dynamic or constitutional mechanism gives the Record that effect and the required conditions obtain.

constitutional Effect ending. It may only project an exact resolved
constitutional judgment through `ConstitutionalEndingProjection`.

### CT11.4 Discontinuity

**Discontinuity under a Criterion** is an established failure of the required continuation relation across a boundary or Interval. It is not a universal absolute unless the Claim explicitly covers every relevant Criterion, which generally cannot be presumed.

Discontinuity may be established by:

- target Ending;
- a prohibited loss;
- loss of a constitutive Relation or organization;
- a disqualifying gap;
- replacement or reconstruction excluded by the Criterion;
- incompatible receiver realization;
- absence of the required derivation bridge under adequate closed coverage.

Failure to prove continuity is not proof of discontinuity.

### CT11.5 Dissolution

**Dissolution** is loss of the organization or relations by which a Whole, System, Relational System, institution, or other organized bearer obtains as that target, while some components, Records, effects, or relations may persist.

A Dissolution Profile must state:

- the constitutive organization;
- the actual dissolution Process or boundary;
- which relations cease;
- which components and residuals remain;
- whether any successor is formed;
- whether institutional or normative Effects also end under Constitution;
- evidence and coverage.

Dispersal of components can dissolve a Whole without destroying each component. Deletion of a structural description does not dissolve its referent.

### CT11.7 Deletion, Erasure, Archival, and Forgetting

Deletion is an actual change to a carrier, index, reference, or system State. It may end accessibility or recoverability of a Record. It does not erase the historical Occurrence represented.

Archival changes storage or access posture. It does not imply Ending of the target or authenticity of the archive. Forgetting is loss or change of Memory capacity or accessible retained differences; it does not make the past unreal.

Protocol erasure, pruning, compaction, garbage collection, and legal deletion requirements each require qualified Profiles. None changes physical History by label alone.

### CT11.8 Death and Domain-Specific Ending

Death is a biology-specific Ending defined by the relevant biological Profile. It is not a universal synonym for Process termination, account closure, System shutdown, model deletion, institutional dissolution, or Continuity failure.

Biological Ending may leave cells, matter, records, effects, descendants, relations, and lineage. Domain-specific Profiles must preserve these distinctions.

### CT11.9 Ending and Succession

A predecessor need not end for a successor relation to obtain unless the Succession Profile requires displacement. A target may end without a successor. A successor may begin before, at, or after predecessor Ending. These temporal relations are explicit fields, not assumptions.

### CT11.10 Ending Without Historical Erasure

Every valid Ending Description preserves:

- that the target existed where established;
- its actual prior States and Occurrences to the extent known;
- the Ending boundary and uncertainty;
- residuals;
- lineage and provenance;
- surviving Records and their limits;
- unresolved successor or continuity questions.

## CT12 — History, Provenance, Origin, and Lineage

### CT12.1 Actual History

Ontology defines History as ordered States and Occurrences attributed to a bearer, System, or Domain. Actual History is not a story, list, database, trace, ledger, archive, or graph, although those may represent selected parts of it.

Continuity uses History to locate how endpoints are related. It does not require complete recording for History to obtain.

### CT12.2 Recorded History

A History Record is selective and physically realized. Its coverage, ordering, attribution, provenance, and correction posture must be explicit.

    authentic Record -/-> complete History
    append-only Record -/-> true content
    immutable protocol entry -/-> physically indestructible carrier
    complete database scan -/-> complete physical History
    missing Record -/-> missing Occurrence

An append-only recording Profile can protect against silent in-place mutation within its mechanism. Append orientation is not the definition of History or Continuity and does not guarantee truth.

### CT12.3 Provenance

Provenance is the actual ordered structure of origin, derivation, custody, transformation, and attribution relations through which a Referent passed.

Continuity distinguishes:

- actual Provenance;
- Provenance Claim;
- Provenance Record;
- evidence about custody or transformation;
- a graph representing selected provenance paths.

Actual Provenance may survive loss of Records. A well-preserved Provenance Record can be false. Provenance establishes neither identity, continuity, Ownership, Authority, truth, nor value by itself.

### CT12.4 Origin

An Origin is a beginning point under an exact causal, derivational, lineage, formation, or reference criterion. A target may have different Origins under different criteria without contradiction.

Examples include:

- material Origin;
- design Origin;
- Form Origin;
- implementation Origin;
- lineage Origin;
- institutional formation Origin;
- current realization Origin;
- record-chain Origin.

No Origin creates permanent privilege, landlord relation, control, Ownership, authority, entitlement, or superior worth. Those require separately constituted relations where they are possible at all.

### CT12.5 Lineage Types

per-source map.

### CT12.9 Custody and Control

Custody is a relation in Provenance. Control is a capacity or relation defined upstream. Neither establishes Ownership, authorship, legitimacy, continuity, or truth. A custody break may damage provenance coverage without ending the target bearer.

### CT12.10 Attribution and Correction

Attribution connects a Claim, Record, Action, or artifact to a purported source or bearer under evidence. Correction creates a later corrective Claim, Record, or State. It must preserve:

- what the earlier Record contained;
- when and by whom or what the correction was made, where attributable;
- the reason and evidence;
- the correction's Scope;
- whether the earlier content was false, incomplete, ambiguous, or merely superseded;
- the actual historical Occurrences independently of either Representation.

Correction without overwrite is a strong recording Profile. It is not the only physically possible correction mechanism and is not metaphysically required for History to exist.

### CT12.11 Lineage Density

Lineage density may be described by qualified measures such as node count, edge count, branching factor, path multiplicity, interaction-linked derivation, retained distinctions, or temporal concentration. Every measure must declare its graph basis, Scope, weighting, uncertainty, and coverage.

Density is descriptive. It does not by itself establish:

- intelligence;
- continuity quality;
- coherence;
- contribution;
- merit;
- economic or evaluative value;
- entitlement or reward;
- Agency or human presence;
- truth.

Emergence may later state a criterion under which some lineage organization participates in an emergent capacity or value relation. Continuity supplies the typed history, not the conclusion.

### CT12.12 Continuity Turn Boundary

A downstream Profile may define a **Continuity Turn** as a bounded Record relating a run, matter, predecessor reference, and actual canonical artifacts. Such a Record can make selected cross-run relations recoverable.

It remains:

- a Representation, not Continuity itself;
- optional outside its Profile;
- unable to replace State, Transition, validation, witness, governance, or other canonical evidence;
- unable to create truth, authority, identity, or value;
- not restricted universally to successful runs;
- required to preserve failed, refused, partial, or nonconforming History where its declared coverage includes them.

The useful predecessor seam is artifact linkage and correction without overwrite. The old success-only emission rule is not universalized.

## CT13 — Composition, Conflict, and Continuity Graphs

### CT13.1 Continuity Does Not Compose Automatically

`SAME` retains Ontology's strict transitivity. `CONTINUES_AS` does not inherit universal transitivity merely because its edges form a path.

Suppose:

    CONTINUES_AS(a, b ; continuity_criterion_and_version = p)
    CONTINUES_AS(b, c ; continuity_criterion_and_version = q)

No relation between a and c follows unless:

- p and q are the same exact Criterion and version, or an explicit mapping permits composition;
- endpoint roles and target aspects align;
- b denotes a compatible intermediate position;
- required invariants survive the whole path;
- admitted losses do not accumulate beyond the composed Criterion;
- gaps and discontinuities remain permitted;
- interval, World, Domain, and Resolution mappings are coherent;
- evidence covers the composed path;
- the applicable Profile declares composition.

`COMPOSITION_ESTABLISHED` establishes only the relation permitted by the mapped Criterion. It does not turn ancestral derivation into direct derivation, `REPLACES` into a transitive relation, `SUCCEEDS` into universal transitivity, or a path of distinct bearers into one numerically identical bearer.

Every `CompositionInputEdge.input_edge_id` equals its map key. For each adjacent
input pair, the first receiver endpoint equals the second source endpoint, one
canonical join key exists, and the ordered intermediate-endpoint list contains
exactly those joins in order. Mapping-reference and mapping-posture maps have
exactly the join-key domain. The temporal map has exactly the union of all
input-edge endpoint IDs, and every temporal value carries the same internal ID
as its key. A single mapping reused across joins is repeated by reference;
heterogeneous mappings remain separately keyed. Any missing, extra, duplicated,
reversed, foreign, or internally mismatched key makes the composition
`ILL_TYPED`.

Each input reference is bound to the displayed edge rather than merely cited by
it. An `ACTUAL_CONTINUES_AS_RELATION_INPUT` must name an actual Relation
Instance whose relation kind is exactly `CONTINUES_AS`, whose ordered relata are
the displayed source and receiver, and whose mandatory Criterion/version and
Scope attributions equal the displayed criterion and the composition Scope. An
`EVALUATED_CONTINUATION_INPUT` must be an evaluated `ContinuationEvaluation`
whose envelope endpoints, Criterion/version, Scope, relation actuality, and
substantive continuation posture equal the displayed edge fields. The edge's
actuality and continuation-posture projections equal the referenced result; a
different relation kind, pair, Criterion, Scope, or posture is `ILL_TYPED`.

`COMPOSITION_ESTABLISHED` requires every input relation to be actual, every
input continuation posture to be established, endpoints, Intervals, and Scopes
to align, every required criterion mapping to be established, cumulative
retention to satisfy the composed Criterion, no disqualifying gap, and adequate
evidence coverage, an applicable exact composition Profile/version, and
`COMPOSED_RELATION_DERIVABLE`. It establishes derivability under that mapped
Criterion; it does not presuppose that an output Relation Instance already
exists. If an output relation is subsequently materialized,
`composed_relation_reference` binds that separately actual Relation Instance
and its relation-actuality judgment. Otherwise it is `NONE`. A profile-only
possible path, unresolved edge, or unevaluated reference cannot support
established composition.

### CT13.5 Multiple Criteria

Different Continuity Criteria may return different results for one history:

- material continuity may fail while formal continuity holds;
- component continuity may fail while System continuity holds;
- Process continuity may fail while service continuity holds;
- bearer persistence may fail while successor continuity holds;
- institutional succession may hold while network continuity remains unresolved;
- State equivalence may hold while strict identity is false.

These are compatible where their target aspects or Criteria differ. An evaluation report must not flatten them into one global continuity score unless a downstream Profile explicitly defines and justifies that aggregation.

### CT13.7 Continuity Graphs and Actuality

A Continuity Graph may represent:

- endpoint positions;
- strict identity Claims;
- continuation relations;
- replacement and succession;
- direct and ancestral derivation;
- branch and merge topology;
- dormancy, recovery, and Ending boundaries;
- provenance and custody;
- uncertainty and conflict.

Each edge must carry its relation kind, Criterion, Scope, Interval, Resolution, actuality posture, evidence coverage, and provenance. A generic unlabeled arrow is insufficient.

An edge's graph field is a **represented actuality posture**. It becomes usable
as an operative actuality premise only through an exact
`RelationActualityBinding` or `ActualBridgeReference` for that same edge. A
Claim, Record, or graph assertion may describe the posture but cannot establish
it.

Graph properties such as connectivity, reachability, centrality, density, depth, component count, path count, or cycle count are properties of the represented graph under its construction. They do not by themselves establish properties of Reality, authority, Agency, intelligence, coherence, or value.

### CT13.8 Path Compression and Summaries

Path compression may create an ancestral or composed edge for efficient representation. It must preserve:

- that the edge is derived rather than direct;
- the underlying path or a recoverable path reference;
- the composition rule;
- accumulated losses and unresolved segments;
- source edge provenance and coverage;
- the distinction between graph optimization and actual lineage.

A summary that cannot recover these distinctions cannot stand as an exact Continuity Record for the compressed relation.

No compressed edge may occupy a direct-edge position. If its underlying path or
composition obligations are not recoverable, it remains a summary
Representation and cannot support an exact operative continuity result.

### CT13.9 Component and Whole Continuity

Component continuity and Whole continuity are assessed separately.

    all components persist -/-> Whole persists
    Whole persists -/-> every component persists
    one constitutive Relation ends -/-> Whole necessarily ends under every Profile
    new components occupy old positions -/-> same Whole

A Whole Profile must state how component, organization, boundary, function, and history results compose. No upward or downward continuity inference is automatic.

### CT13.10 Distributed-System Continuity

A distributed System may persist through node replacement, Process restart, network partition, leader change, or failover where its Profile permits those changes and the required organization, State relations, and History basis remain.

Continuity must distinguish:

- distributed System bearer;
- nodes and validators;
- active Processes;
- replicated State;
- consensus history;
- canonical branch Status;
- protocol or Form version;
- operator and controller relations;
- institutional or constitutional source.

Consensus agreement or ledger finality does not prove System identity. The same validators can instantiate a new network. Different validators may continue one network under a Network Profile. A copied ledger State can initialize a new formation with ancestry.

### CT13.11 Relational Continuity

A Relation or Relational System may persist while participants change if its constitutive organization and History satisfy the applicable Profile. Conversely, persistent participants do not keep an ended Relation alive.

Relation-local State, participant State, and records about the Relation remain separate. A proposed or merely recorded Relation cannot acquire History by graph construction alone.

### CT13.12 Cross-Scale Continuity

Continuity Claims may cross scales only through explicit scale mappings. Molecular turnover, cellular persistence, organism continuity, role continuity, social continuity, institutional continuity, and ecosystem continuity are distinct. A result at one scale neither automatically determines nor is reducible to a result at another.

### CT13.13 Cross-World Continuity

A cross-World Continuity Claim requires:

- separately identified World Contexts;
- endpoint reference mappings;
- bearer and realization placement in each World;
- an actual bridge or derivation basis where claimed;
- a Criterion able to span the Worlds;
- explicit loss, interpretation, and representation mappings;
- no identity inference from equal labels, hashes, or positions.

Simulated, represented, mirrored, or imported entities do not become one cross-World bearer by correspondence alone.

### CT13.14 Cycles

A cycle in a Continuity Graph can mean:

- repeated reference to one persistent bearer;
- a representational or versioning loop;
- circular provenance Claims;
- reintroduction of derived content;
- invalid direct-derivation topology;
- a domain-permitted recurrent lineage;
- graph error.

Each relation kind declares whether cycles are permitted. No cycle establishes self-causation, identity, or continuity automatically.

### CT13.15 Composite Assessment Closure

A composite continuity result is well formed only where every contributing relation and mapping is typed, actual or explicitly unresolved, and Scope-compatible. Any unknown intermediate segment remains visible. Aggregation cannot convert an unresolved required segment into an established relation.

## CT14 — Observation, Evidence, and Conformance

### CT14.1 Observation Does Not Stand Outside Continuity

Observation is an actual Process with a bearer, instrument or capacity, target position, method, Interval, Resolution, and possible error. It samples or detects selected conditions. It neither creates nor exhausts the continuity relation observed.

An observer may begin or cease observing while the target persists. Repeated observation may disturb the target. Absence of detection is interpreted only under the method's coverage and sensitivity.

### CT14.2 Evidence Families

Continuity Evidence may include:

- direct observations of endpoint bearers;
- structural Configurations and realization bases;
- dynamic traces of change, maintenance, copy, branch, merge, suspension, recovery, or Ending;
- physical continuity of matter or organization;
- causal and derivational traces;
- retained State and invariant measurements;
- provenance and custody evidence;
- records, signatures, checksums, identifiers, and timestamps;
- constitutional judgments for exact institutional questions;
- testimony or self-report as typed Claims;
- negative evidence under adequate closed coverage.

No Evidence family is universally decisive. Its force depends on the target Kind, Criterion, Scope, method, provenance, and alternatives excluded.

### CT14.3 Actuality and Relation Assessment

An assessor must separately place:

1. each endpoint's actuality;
2. each endpoint's realization where material;
3. the bridge Occurrences or observational gap;
4. each retained, transformed, duplicated, reconstructed, and lost item;
5. strict identity evidence;
6. the obtaining of each claimed Relation Instance;
7. the truth of Claims and fidelity of Records;
8. Profile applicability;
9. Profile conformance.

Evidence for one position does not automatically satisfy another.

### CT14.4 Continuity Description Truth Posture

A Continuity Description can be:

- supported while incomplete;
- unsupported while authentic as a Record;
- partly accurate with unresolved edges;
- superseded by a later correction without historical deletion;
- false despite conformance to a schema;
- true despite later loss of its carrier.

Schema validity, signature validity, protocol inclusion, consensus, finality, or append-only storage does not equal truth.

### CT14.5 Record Well-Formedness

    WF_CONTINUITY_RECORD(cr)
      -> WELL_FORMED | ILL_TYPED

`WELL_FORMED` requires:

1. a physically realized carrier and format;
2. exact `RepresentationBinding`;
3. target endpoint and relation references;
4. Criterion and version;
5. Scope, Interval, Resolution, and coverage;
6. method and evidence references;
7. result dimensions without collapse;
8. provenance and attribution;
9. correction or supersession references;
10. recoverability conditions;
11. no assertion that recordation created the represented continuity.

Record well-formedness does not establish authenticity, truth, continuity, or conformance of the represented target.

The procedure is:

1. establish a well-typed request;
2. evaluate Profile applicability;
3. establish the actuality of the target relation, bearer history, or other operative target required by p;
4. evaluate each applicable Profile condition;
5. preserve mixed or unresolved dimensions;
6. return one Ontology conformance result.

`CONFORMS` means the actual target satisfies every applicable requirement of p in CS. It does not make the target actual, true in every description, valid under Constitution, numerically identical across endpoints, or valuable. `DOES_NOT_CONFORM` does not erase the actual nonconforming history. `UNRESOLVED` is used where an actual applicable target cannot yet be conclusively classified.

If target actuality is disproved or unresolved, conformance is `NOT_EVALUATED` except where the Profile explicitly evaluates a negative target-position claim. An ill-typed request returns external `ILL_TYPED`.

The three aggregate tokens are exact imported Ontology conformance-result
members, not Continuity-owned redeclarations. External `ILL_TYPED` completes
Ontology's four-class grammar before an evaluation envelope is constructed.
The requirement map has exactly the applicable Profile-requirement domain and
each inner ID equals its key. `CONFORMS` requires established target actuality
and every applicable requirement to conform. `DOES_NOT_CONFORM` requires
established target actuality and at least one adequately evidenced negative
requirement. `UNRESOLVED` applies where no adequate negative is established and
at least one applicable requirement remains unresolved. The aggregate is a
deterministic projection of that closed map and cannot hide mixed dimensions.

### CT14.7 Assessment Provenance

Every assessment must preserve:

- assessor or producing mechanism attribution where established;
- Criterion and Profile bytes or exact binding;
- implementation and version where computational;
- input Evidence and assumptions;
- method;
- time and Scope of assessment;
- coverage;
- output and reason codes;
- later correction or contestation;
- unresolved conflicts.

Assessment provenance supports audit. It does not create epistemic or normative Authority by itself.

### CT14.8 Correction and Supersession

A later assessment may correct, refine, or supersede an earlier assessment. It must not silently overwrite the earlier historical result.

The later assessment identifies:

- the earlier assessment;
- whether the issue was typing, evidence, method, Criterion version, implementation, or interpretation;
- which result dimensions change;
- new evidence and method;
- whether the target history changed or only knowledge about it changed;
- remaining unresolved positions.

If only the judgment Representation was ill-typed, a later well-typed assessment may concern the same historical Occurrences. If the underlying purported operation never occurred, the later assessment cannot make it have occurred.

### CT14.9 Human Source Form and Machine Projection

This exact human-readable Form is the semantic source for this layer. A schema, ontology serialization, code generator, evaluator, graph database, index, compiled policy, model prompt, or machine-readable twin is a derivative Representation unless a separately valid succession route establishes otherwise.

A machine projection must preserve:

- every Kind and relation signature;
- every result family and unresolved posture;
- strict identity firewall;
- endpoint, bridge, Criterion, and Scope separation;
- retention and loss distinctions;
- constitutional and emergence boundaries;
- exact source binding and version;
- errors rather than silent coercion.

Successful parsing or execution does not prove semantic fidelity.

### CT14.10 Adversarial Conformance Fixtures

| Fixture | Required placement |
|---|---|

## CT15 — Open Continuity Boundary

### CT15.1 Continuity Placement

An encountered continuity question need not fit an existing mode or Profile before it can be preserved for examination. The layer must distinguish:

- an unresolved endpoint's existence;
- an unresolved endpoint Kind;
- a known endpoint with unknown bridge history;
- a known history with no adequate Criterion;
- an applicable Criterion with insufficient Evidence;
- conflicting Criteria or evidence;
- an unfamiliar continuity mode;
- a malformed question.

### CT15.4 Interface to Substrate

Substrate constrains every physically realized endpoint, carrier, memory trace, Record, Copy, bridge Process, retained basis, and recovery mechanism.

Continuity cannot:

- continue a bearer through established physical nonexistence under strict persistence;
- make a representation physically indestructible;
- treat information as carrierless;
- recover distinctions not physically retained or otherwise realized;
- reverse an actual irreversible history by declaration.

Substrate does not decide higher-level continuity Criteria by itself. Physical constituent change may or may not be material to an organism, System, or Form Profile.

### CT15.5 Interface to Ontology

Ontology supplies:

- existence and obtaining;
- strict `SAME` and `DIFFERENT`;
- Bearer, State, Process, Relation, System, Form, Pattern, Role, and Status;
- Copy, History, Provenance, Lineage, Origin, Beginning, and Ending;
- equivalence, representation, realization, evidence, conformance, and open placement;
- `CONTINUES_AS` as reserved surface relation.

Continuity supplies:

- the qualified relation grammar for `CONTINUES_AS`;
- persistence and continuation Criteria;
- preservation, transformation, and loss manifests;
- replacement, Copy, migration, branch, merge, succession, dormancy, recovery, discontinuity, and Ending assessments;
- composition limits and continuity-specific unresolved placement.

Continuity does not amend Ontology. Any conflict returns `ILL_TYPED`, `UNRESOLVED`, or nonconformance as appropriate; it is not repaired by redefining an imported term.

### CT15.6 Interface to Constitution

Constitution owns:

- valid Adoption, Amendment, Repeal, Ending, and constitutional Succession;
- Currentness and applicability of constitutional Forms;
- institutional formation and Status;
- Subject Binding, Consent reliance, Authorization, Authority, Right, Obligation, Responsibility, Review, Remedy, and constitutional Effect;
- constitutional `CARRIES(...)` and its exact predecessor inventory.

Continuity owns descriptive persistence and relation across change.

Therefore:

- valid constitutional succession may be an input to an institutional succession assessment but cannot prove physical, technical, System, Process, Agent, Subject, or numerical continuity;
- descriptive continuity cannot validate a constitutional Act or make a Form Current;
- normative Carry does not prove physical retention;
- physical copying of a right, permission, key, or obligation Record does not transfer the normative Relation;
- an ended constitutional Effect can leave historical Records, consequences, and descriptive lineage;
- a continuing institution may still lack valid present Authority;
- a newly valid institution need not be numerically identical to a predecessor.

Where constitutional and continuity results differ, both remain visible in their proper domains.

### CT15.7 Interface to Structure

Structure supplies:

- actual endpoint Bearers and positions;
- Configurations, components, relations, Boundaries, Interfaces, Worlds, Systems, and Relational Systems;
- replacement Slots, paths, mappings, realization bases, and structural coverage;
- snapshots and records without persistence conclusions.

Continuity supplies:

- whether a bearer or System persists across Configurations;
- whether replacement preserves whole continuity;
- relation-local and Relational-System persistence/history bases;
- branch, merge, succession, dormancy, recovery, dissolution, and Ending relations;
- cross-World and cross-scale continuation only through explicit mappings.

Continuity does not retroactively create the structural endpoint it evaluates. Structure may use an established Continuity result as evidence for later history-qualified conformance without creating a dependency cycle in the earlier snapshot or Configuration description.

### CT15.8 Interface to Dynamics

Dynamics supplies:

- exact prior and subsequent State and Configuration positions;
- actual Transition Occurrences and Processes;
- reconfiguration and maintenance;
- suspension, resumption, recovery, and Ending lifecycle results;
- copy crossing, source-retention, receiver-realization, preservation, transformation, and loss results;
- actual forks, reconciliations, conflicts, commitments, and finality;
- context closure, teardown, replay, repair, rollback, and compensation;
- ordered traces, provenance Claims, evidence coverage, and unresolved dynamic positions.

Continuity classifies relations across these actualities. It does not:

- make a possible path traversed;
- make a Transition occur;
- turn retry into one Process;
- treat rollback as erased History;
- infer identity from a State comparison;
- make consensus or finality establish continuity;
- make operational recovery prove resilience.

An actual dynamic history can be fully known while continuity remains unresolved because the governing Criterion or identity basis is open.

### CT15.9 Interface to Emergence

Continuity passes downstream:

- established and unresolved persistence and `CONTINUES_AS` relations;
- independently supported strict identity postures without redefining identity;
- retained, transformed, reconstructed, duplicated, and lost distinctions;
- replacement, Copy, migration, branch, merge, and succession topology;
- dormancy, interruption, resumption, recovery, Ending, and dissolution results;
- actual histories, provenance, Origins, Lineages, conflicts, gaps, and uncertainty;
- relation-local, System, distributed, cross-scale, and cross-World continuity results;
- descriptive lineage measures and their coverage.

Emergence alone decides whether those relations realize:

- memory as an effective capacity;
- life or biological autonomy;
- Self or self-maintenance;
- cognition;
- Agency or Intention;
- Experience, consciousness, or Subject status;
- intelligence;
- meaning;
- adaptation, robustness, or resilience;
- coherence as an effective organization;
- collective capacity;
- contribution, evaluative value, economic value, or lineage-density significance.

Long continuity does not equal high value. Dense lineage does not equal intelligence. Recovery does not equal resilience. Preserved State does not equal memory in the cognitive sense. A continuing system does not thereby become a Self.

Downstream Profiles may define exact continuity regimes for:

- persons, Agents, or Subjects once those target Kinds are independently established;
- documents, constitutional Forms, and records;

- local Worlds, kernels, Centres, or other project-specific structural positions;

Every qualified Profile must preserve the universal grammar. It may specialize; it may not redefine strict identity, erase History, self-authorize succession, silently transfer normative relations, or install value by naming.

### CT15.12 The Continuity Ground That Remains

What changes belongs to Dynamics.  
What is arranged belongs to Structure.  
What is one bearer belongs to Ontology's strict identity.  
What remains related across change belongs here.  

A copied form is not its source.  
A successor is not automatically the same bearer.  
A sleeping bearer is not necessarily gone.  
A restored State is not the earlier moment.  
A branch does not split numerical identity.  
A merge does not fuse pasts.  
An Ending does not erase a History.  
A record does not create the relation it records.  

Continuity is exact when it says **which relation survives, under which criterion, across which actual change, with what preserved, transformed, lost, or unresolved—and no more**.

This is the complete universal continuity grammar presently required before particular Worlds, organisms, institutions, networks, Agents, Subjects, or L1s can receive their own continuity Profiles.
